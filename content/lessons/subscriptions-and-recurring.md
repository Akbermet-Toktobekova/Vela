# Auditing Subscriptions and Recurring Charges

Digital services, streaming platforms, fitness memberships, and software trials increasingly rely on automatic monthly or annual recurring billing. Because individual charges often appear modest—such as €7.99, €12.99, or €19.99—they can easily slip by unnoticed on bank statements.

Over time, several forgotten or under-utilized subscriptions accumulate into a substantial monthly outflow. For instance, four unused services costing €12 each silently drain €576 every year from your cash capacity.

Conducting a periodic subscription audit is a calm, judgment-free review of your statement history. Group your recurring charges into three clear categories:
1. **Essential utilities** (internet, mobile service, core work tools).
2. **Actively valued services** that you use frequently and genuinely appreciate.
3. **Passive or dormant charges** from services you haven't opened in over 30 days.

For services in the third group, consider canceling or pausing them. Remember that canceling a digital subscription is rarely permanent; if you discover you miss the service next month, you can easily resubscribe. In the meantime, the reclaimed €25 or €50 per month immediately strengthens your available buffer.

### Quick Check

**Question:** What is the most effective approach to handling recurring subscriptions you haven't used recently?
- [ ] Keep paying in case you might want to use them six months from now
- [x] Cancel or pause them, knowing you can easily reactivate if genuinely needed later
- [ ] Ignore bank statements to avoid making decisions

**Explanation:** Canceling inactive subscriptions immediately stops cash leakage with zero long-term penalty, as virtually all modern digital services allow instant reactivation.
