# Liquidity Buffer vs Long-Term Savings

A common financial mistake is confusing your checking account balance with money that is entirely free to spend. In reality, your current balance must cover three distinct tiers: committed bills, a liquidity buffer, and true discretionary cash.

A liquidity buffer (also called an unallocated balance or breathing room) is a modest cushion—typically €100 to €200—left permanently in your checking account. Its purpose is not long-term wealth building, but preventing accidental overdrafts caused by timing mismatches. For example, if your utility bill of €80 is debited two days before your expected payday, a liquidity buffer absorbs the debit smoothly with zero overdraft fees or bounced payments.

Long-term savings, by contrast, are moved out of your checking account toward specific future goals or reserves. If you allocate all your remaining cash into a locked savings account on the 10th of the month, you risk having to transfer it right back when a minor bill clears on the 14th.

Always protect a baseline buffer first. Only money that remains above your upcoming bills and your baseline buffer is considered true discretionary capacity eligible for goal contributions.
