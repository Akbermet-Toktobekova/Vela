# APR and the True Cost of Minimum Payments

Annual Percentage Rate (APR) represents the yearly cost of borrowing money, including standard interest and recurring mandatory fees. When you carry a balance on a credit card or personal loan with an APR of 18% or 24%, interest accrues monthly on the outstanding balance.

Lenders typically set a mandatory "minimum payment"—often calculated as the monthly interest plus a tiny fraction (1% or 2%) of the principal, or a flat minimum such as €25. While paying the minimum keeps your account in good standing and avoids late fees, it is engineered to stretch repayment over years or even decades.

For example, consider a balance of €1,000 at 20% APR. If you only pay a minimum of €25 each month, more than €16 of your first payment goes purely toward interest charges, reducing the actual debt by less than €9. Over the life of the loan, you could end up paying hundreds of euros in interest charges alone.

Whenever your budget allows even a small additional payment—such as €20 or €30 above the minimum—that extra amount goes directly to reducing the principal balance. This immediately lowers the interest charged next month and accelerates your debt-free date.
